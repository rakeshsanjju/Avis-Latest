Modern RFA Technique for Safe and Quick Recovery

Radio Frequency Ablation (RFA) is a minimally invasive procedure adopted to treat varicose veins. This procedure is performed to avoid pain and follow nonsurgical technique with application of radiofrequency energy for generating heat. The heat is sent into the vein through a thin, flexible tube (catheter). This closes off blood flow in the main problem vein.

RFA is a very effective procedure for targeted treatment towards specific abnormal tissue, with relatively low risk of complications such as bleeding, infection and damage to nearby tissue or organ. RFA has got applications especially for treating varicose veins, shrinking of benign thyroid nodules, and certain cases of endometriosis with difficult location-to-reach areas.

Treatments and Solutions

Regular exercise to boost circulation.
Maintain a healthy weight to reduce vein strain.
Avoid prolonged standing or sitting—take breaks to move.
Elevate legs periodically during the day.
Eat a high-fiber, low-salt diet to minimize vein pressure.
Wear comfortable clothing and shoes to support vascular health.

How is Rf Ablation performed: Before, during and after

Before surgery: The doctor will numb the area either with local anaesthesia or general anaesthesia. It depends on the condition of the patient.

During surgery: A tiny incision is made on the skin. It is made in the area where the electrode needs to be placed. Through the incision, a laparoscopic plastic tube is inserted. Using the guidance of a camera, the needle electrodes are inserted at the tumour site. Once the procedure is done, the electrodes are removed, and the incision is closed. This procedure is known as laparoscopy.

After surgery: The patient may feel drowsiness or slight pain later.
